# 0004: One Era, One Protagonist, One Causal Chain

Supersedes the act list in ADR-0001 and the per-act citations in ADR-0003. The title 《定分止爭錄》 from ADR-0003 stands.

The first edition spanned Song and Ming, gave the player an invented office (算學提刑官), and left the three acts unconnected, so there was no answer to who the protagonist is, why he appears in each scene, or why a minor official may speak before the emperor. The second edition sets all three acts in the Wanli reign (1574–1581) and follows one fictional official, Cheng Heng, through real Ming offices: Prefectural Judge in Jinhua, transferred to Hangzhou, then promoted to Supervising Secretary in Beijing.

Each act causes the next. The Yiwu case reaches Qi Jiguang through a veteran's letter; the Hangzhou case serves both Zhang Juzheng's land survey and Feng Bao's weaving office; all three therefore accept Cheng Heng in Act III, where he enters by sending back a one-sided edict and is ordered to draft a Proposal. He never decides for the emperor: he proposes, and the emperor rules.

## Considered Options

- **Keep the Song first act for economic-history variety.** Rejected: a dynasty change breaks the single-protagonist storyline and doubles the art direction for cutscenes.
- **Make the protagonist a high official or imperial favourite.** Rejected: the Supervising Secretary's power to send back edicts is a real institution that explains a rank-7 voice at court without inventing one.

## Consequences

- Real figures (Wanli, Zhang Juzheng, Qi Jiguang, Feng Bao) appear only in Act III; all other characters are fictional.
- Endings are written against the real events of 1582–1584, so one ending (鼎足而立) is deliberately counterfactual.
- Dialogue uses plain modern Chinese with period forms of address; institutional terms appear only with an in-line gloss. This amends the classical court-drama tone of ADR-0002.
